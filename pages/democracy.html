some thoughts on democracy

i believe in good government reforms, that is, laws and policies that increase transparency into the government and make it more democractic: reflective of the opinions held by the public.

things i'm for

  1. lower population per congressional representative (close to original numbers: ~30,000 per rep)
  2. ranked choice voting
  3. more progressive taxing of ultrawealthy income and a hard upper limit on permitted individual net worth as a percentage of GDP (say, 1 tenth of 1 percent. this would affect about 30 people in the USA)

the purpose of government

i am not sure about how involved government should be in the welfare of individuals. however, i do not believe government should exist solely to protect capital. this kind of system, a.k.a. unregulated capitalism, is already exploitative of labor and creates an unnessecary level of suffering for labor for a reward of marginal productivity increases. in fact, if the history of American labor uprisings in the late 19th and early 20th century can teach us anything, it may be that over-exploitation of labor may be a huge net negative due to strikes, exhaustion, and the general disillusionment of poorly treated workers. in my view, capitalism is a tool for resource distribution that is highly useful, but too harsh in its raw form to be left unregulated. capitalism fits into a larger, more complex societal system for rewarding innovation and punishing inefficiency.

that's a pretty crazy idea for a wealth tax

capitalism, approximately, is a societal framework that gives power to people with money. in theory, people who are smart and/or useful to society are rewarded for their usefulness with money, which they can use in turn to enjoy a higher standard of living. if an individual or a corporation accumulates enough money, they can even popularize and perpetuate those useful actions, goods, or services, making them more common in the society, which is all achieved with money. money is power. this is fine. however, if a single person or company has enough money, they can gain enough power to not only popularize ideas and behaviors, but force everyone else to follow their personal philosophy. my opinion is that the accumulation of money should be limited when influence turns into control.

we see this happening today with technology companies that act almost as direct proxies for their billionaire/trillionaire CEOs: Meta with Zuck, OpenAI with Altman, Oracle/Paramount with the Ellisons, Palantir with Thiel, of course Tesla/SpaceX with Musk, and many others. do we want our country to become the governed by a megacorporation oligarchy, like the dystopian future described in Marie Lu's Legend trilogy? if not, there must be a limit to what proportion of the nation's wealth is controlled by a single actor.
